Markup and profit margin are not the same thing

Adding 20% to cost is not earning 20% of the sale. A common calculation mistake in fabrication.

Author: DoorWin Editor (sample author) · Published: 2026-10-20

Short answer: Markup is calculated on cost, margin on the sale price. Add 20% to a cost of 1,000 and the sale is 1,200, but margin is 200/1,200, about 16.7%.

Why it matters

Discounts come off the sale. A 10% discount on a 1,200 quote is 120, which removes 60% of the 200 profit.

Tracking

Follow net cost, sale and profit together on every project, and set your negotiation room before opening the quote.

Questions and answers

How do I find margin?

Divide profit by sale price: 200 ÷ 1,200 = 16.7%.

How do I price for a target margin?

Divide cost by (1 − margin): 1,000 ÷ 0.80 = 1,250.
