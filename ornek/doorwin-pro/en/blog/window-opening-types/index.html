Window opening types: fixed, casement, tilt and sliding

The triangle on the drawing is there to be read: its tip marks the hinge side. Four types and what each does to the price.

Author: DoorWin Editor (sample author) · Published: 12 October 2026

Drawing rule: the triangle’s tip marks the hinge side, with the handle on the opposite edge. On a tilt window the tip points down, because the sash hinges at the bottom.

The four types

TypeGood forEffect on the quote
FixedViews, high façadesNo sash or hardware; cheapest
TurnFloors that need cleaning accessSash profile and opening set
Tilt and turnRooms needing ventilationCostlier hardware set
SlidingNarrow balconies, wide openingsRail set, sash weight

Most often misread

On a double sash, hinges sit at the outer edges and the handle at the middle. Leave direction off the quote and the customer finds out on opening day. Note it when you take the measure.

Questions and answers

Why is sliding priced separately?

Sliding systems use a track and their own profile set, so they are listed separately.

Is fixed glazing cheaper?

With no sash or opening hardware, the same size usually has fewer cost lines.
